# Census of Anton Brelochs’s annual prognostications

20 November 2026 · Forecast years 1528–1559 · [Year-by-year census](forecast-years.csv) · [Editions, copies and source links](witnesses.csv) · [Reassessment of the polemic](../analysis/CENSUS-REASSESSMENT.md)

**Twenty-six of the 32 forecast years have a specific bibliographic attestation.** Twenty-four have a named collection or a copy described by a modern bibliographer. Two have only an explicit entry in a nineteenth-century observatory catalogue. Twelve now have a readable opening text in the project, including the French address for 1533. Readable German dedications cover eleven years. These are counts of distinct years. Two languages for 1533, two German editions for 1542, or multiple copies of the same setting contribute one year each.

The earlier working set included the eleven German texts and a title-page lead for 1548. The new register documents **fourteen further years beyond that baseline**. Several had already appeared on the project’s list of years to seek. Survival for the six open years remains undetermined. The forecast year printed or reported in the title takes precedence over the printing year, the dedication date and modern digitization metadata. For example, a publication dated 1537 can forecast 1538, and the Heidelberg publication dated 1556 forecasts 1557. The four items in Green’s Nuremberg volume and the separately documented 1556 forecast show what a search confined to VD16 can miss.

## Other editions, genres and disputed dates

- **Letovice, reported for 1535; possible 1525 reading.** Petr Mašek describes a 19-item volume from the Letovice castle library. He assigns Brelochs’s item to **1535**, explicitly distinguishes it from Philipp Ulhart’s Augsburg VD16 B 7417 and describes a Venus, Jupiter and Saturn woodcut. His n. 12 transcribes the date as **M.D.xxv.**, i.e. **1525**. The context and the comparison favor 1535, but a photograph of the title leaf, internal year and colophon could establish an earlier forecast or a printing error. No separate 1525 year is counted. No equivalence with the project’s two Augsburg-setting dedications is assumed. [Mašek, pp. 5–6 and n. 12](https://publikace.nm.cz/file/75e36dfa55bddf1df25757cd048a4aeb/17727/ISSN%200036-5351%201-2%202012%20p.%205-8.pdf)
- **Other copies and formats.** Herbst identifies a fragment of 1529 in Kiel, Cb 6872, alongside the complete WLS copy. For 1538 there is a Leipzig digitization as well as Heidelberg. Vienna has a digitization for 1559, 72.F.45.(5). These are leads for collation within years already represented. The 1545 pocket almanac in Staatsarchiv Nürnberg, Rep. 129, Nr. 248/2, has an appended abbreviated practica. That combination should be checked separately from the quarto edition. [Herbst](https://www.presseforschung.uni-bremen.de/dokuwiki/doku.php?id=brelochs_anton) · [FAU record](https://schreibkalender.wisski.data.fau.de/wisski/navigate/84192/view) · [Description of the abridgment](https://www.db-thueringen.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/dbt_derivate_00063802/Kalender_3_4.pdf)
- **Scope at the edges.** Herbst gives 1528 as the earliest transmitted practica and 1559 as Anton’s last documented authorial year. The 1529 plague book (VD16 B 7414), 1531 comet tract (B 7425), annual wall calendars, and writing calendars are recorded as different genres. The 1563 almanac under Anton’s name (ZV 32265) is digitized. Herbst, citing Matthäus, reports use of Anton’s name down to 1568, and identifies his son Sebastian as continuing the family’s work. Posthumous branding and Sebastian’s own signed works require a separate attribution history. [Herbst](https://www.presseforschung.uni-bremen.de/dokuwiki/doku.php?id=brelochs_anton) · [1563 scan](https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb00142219) · [Sebastian’s signed 1565 forecast](https://www.zvdd.de/dms/load/met/?PPN=PPN800483081)
- **Geographical and digitization limits.** French transmission is securely attested for 1533 and 1549. The Lyon title alone cannot supply a German edition or establish continuity of the Geneva preface. Separate publication places, languages, patrons and formats can affect a prologue. Each inaccessible opening is recorded as unknown. The six year gaps should be used as catalogue-search targets, never as evidence that Brelochs stopped writing or stopped defending astrology.
