# The 1548 gap: new primary evidence and a sharper test

Research continuation, 24 October 2026. This supplements REPORT.md; the preface corpus still comprises eleven forecast years.

## What was recovered

A public photograph of **A1r, the title page of Brelochs's practica for 1548**, is available through the University of Warsaw's URUS research database. The image has been retrieved and visually inspected. It identifies Anton Brelochs, the forecast year 1548, his medical and arts credentials, his appointment at Schwäbisch Hall, and the printer Christoph Gutknecht at Nuremberg. Its three principal woodcuts are labeled Jupiter, Mars and Saturn; an additional black disk is labeled as an eclipse of the moon.

The copy record identifies **Wolfenbüttel, Herzog August Bibliothek, A: 125.34 Quod. (7)**. This is concordant with the existing bibliographic lead. The URUS impression record identifies its page as A1r and exposes one illustration image. It does not supply A1v or the rest of the booklet. The preceding bound item's visible sliver on the left of the photograph cannot be treated as the 1548 preface.

The URUS copy record assigns publication to 1547. VD16 also catalogs the printing as 1547. The photographed title page gives the forecast year, but no separately visible imprint year. Thus 1547 remains catalog evidence, not a newly read date in this image. The bound-volume identifier and the trailing handwritten item number are useful identification aids; neither supplies an argument about astrology.

Credit the holding institution and the URUS project when referring to the photograph. The public image record inspected here did not supply an explicit reuse license; no open license is asserted for the modern photograph.

## Why the title page adds something

Beneath the woodcuts the page carries an imperial and royal privilege formula, including a prohibition on reprinting: “Mit Keyserlicher vnd Königklicher May. / Freyheyt/nit nach zu drucken verbotten”. This creates a useful distinction between three kinds of authority:

| Authority | Evidence currently available | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Professional | Author identifies himself by arts and medical credentials and civic appointment | Self-presentation; does not establish that physicians as a group approved every prediction |
| Legal/publication | Printed privilege formula and reprinting prohibition | No privilege instrument inspected; the formula does not establish approval of the book's theological claims |
| Epistemic/theological | Green reports a defense against necromancy on A1v | A1v not yet recovered; the author's actual wording and relation to critics remain unknown |

This is a prospective control on the interpretation of the missing preface. Even if A1v defends natural causal knowledge, the title page can simultaneously appeal to professional office and publication privilege. Those dimensions should be coded separately rather than combined into a single measure of legitimacy.

## What the search did not establish

No publicly accessible image of Brelochs's 1548 A1v was recovered in this continuation. That does not establish that the book is undigitized. Search results for a similarly titled **Practica Teutsch auf das 1548. Jare**, including MDZ identifier **bsb10198896**, led to material attributed to **Dionysius Sibenbürger**, not Brelochs. Those results must not be used to fill this corpus gap. The URUS photograph is positively identified as Brelochs by its own printed title.

The cached VD16 record names Wolfenbüttel as a holding and describes eight leaves, quarto, printed at Nuremberg by Christoph Gutknecht. Herbst additionally lists Erlangen. The Erlangen shelfmark has not been established here. In particular, **Trew S 96 must not be assigned to Brelochs** from a search snippet spanning adjacent entries in Green: that shelfmark belongs to the neighboring Brotbeihel lead.

Green's relevant argument is on printed p. 132, with the source citation at p. 234, chapter 6, note 5 (VD16 ZV 2416, a1v). This is a specific primary-source pointer, but remains secondary evidence until the leaf is inspected.

## A test that can change the conclusion

The next reading should classify the actual form of the 1548 passage before interpreting its historical meaning:

| If A1v contains… | Initial classification | Consequence for the hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| A report that others accuse the author or astrologers of necromancy | E: explicit represented accusation | Adds a specific alleged objection beyond the vague contempt of 1542; still requires independent corroboration |
| A conditional formula, such as what would be wrong if the art were necromancy | R: staged objection | Establishes a defensive possibility, not an external accusation |
| A positive claim that the art is natural, without reporting a charge | I: inferred objection | Green's category may describe an implied polemic rather than a reported controversy |
| A reference to a named opponent, publication or dated encounter | E plus identification | Enables a direct comparison with independently surviving criticism |
| Wording repeated in earlier or later annual prefaces | Reuse flag in addition to E/R/I | Weakens the inference that the criticism first appeared in 1548 |

“Necromancy” itself needs historical lexical control. Record the exact German term before deciding whether Brelochs or his opponent means illicit divination, demonic arts, consultation of the dead, or a broader category of forbidden knowledge. Green's English category should not predetermine that result.

The first chapter should then be read with the preface. Does it merely list planetary configurations, or explicitly present those configurations as the publicly checkable grounds of prediction? The distinction matters: listing observations alone does not demonstrate that all inferred earthly outcomes follow from them, and it does not tell us what an opponent actually disputed.

The decisive diachronic question is therefore narrower: **Does the series show a new, specifically formulated challenge to the source or permissibility of astrological knowledge, or a different deployment of an established defense of natural knowledge?** The retrieved title page helps anchor the object of study, but cannot settle that question.

## Reproduction request prepared, not sent

The minimum useful request is A1r–A2v, including A1v and the first chapter or its beginning. If the opening argument or chapter continues, request the continuation. Since VD16 describes only eight leaves, images of the entire booklet may be more efficient if readily available. Preserve original sequence, blanks and catchwords for identifying boundaries.

A German request draft has been prepared locally and excluded from the public repository. No message or reproduction order has been sent. Paid services have not been ordered.
