## Classification clarification

The primary-only reader used a broad category for explicitly staged distinctions and questions. It included the 1528 nonnecessity disclaimer, the 1542 noncompulsion disclaimer, and the 1558 qualifications. The initial register used R more narrowly for an expressly constructed objection/concession and I when the *external accusation* was inferred from a defense. These are different levels of description, not competing readings of whether the words are explicit.

The new objection register therefore records **authorial form and critic attribution in separate columns**. In particular, O05 records the explicit disclaimer in 1542 while classing an external determinist accusation as inferred; O09 records explicit causal qualification in 1558 while classing a prediction-failure objection as inferred. No formal disagreement rate has been inferred from these differently framed categories.

## Refinements adopted

- Separate theological freedom, human noncompulsion and obstructible terrestrial effects, rather than treating all qualifications as one undifferentiated anti-determinism.
- Include the rhetorical “we/us” in 1547; it complicates a clean author-versus-external-critics model.
- Explain that reused argument can remain contemporarily relevant. Reuse limits independence and claims of novelty; it does not demonstrate unchanged external circumstances.
- Compare the magnet's different argumentative functions in 1542 and 1558: hidden efficacy versus the obstruction of an effect.
- Use dedication dates when proposing a relation to historical events, rather than treating forecast years as composition dates.
- Retain the ambiguity of the 1558 counteracting-cause phrase. The clearer following sentence supports the broad claim without requiring an exact physical interpretation.
- Treat the uncertain 1528 A2v2–3 clause as unnecessary to the prevention finding; A2v5–6 supplies a clear independent formulation within the same preface.
