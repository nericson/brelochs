# Independent primary-text reading: Brelochs's polemical positions

This is an independent AI reading, not human inter-rater reliability. I read the German dedication text in every TEI in `dedicatory-prefaces/tei/`. I did not consult existing analysis, reports, coding, or translations. Source IDs below are the TEIs' `lb/@xml:id`; leaf names are their `pb/@n`. The TEIs identify themselves as provisional first-pass transcriptions, with independent human collation outstanding. The claims below are therefore claims about these transcribed texts, not independently collated print witnesses.

The two 1535 files have identical dedication bodies in this transcription. I treat them as one textual occasion, leaving 11 distinct preface texts from 12 TEI files. They should not supply two observations in a time series. Dates in the filenames are forecast years; the printed signing dates often fall in the preceding year.

## Categories

- **A — Explicit report of criticism/reception:** Brelochs represents other people as expressing an adverse judgment or treating astronomy/practica disparagingly. This establishes an authorial report, not the truth, independence, prevalence, or exact historical wording of the alleged response.
- **B — Explicitly staged possible objection or corrective distinction:** Brelochs formulates a proposition, counterfactual, question, or qualification in his own discourse. He need not attribute it to actual critics. A disclaimer alone is not evidence somebody had made the corresponding charge.
- **C — Inferred objection:** An analyst infers a possible concern answered by a positive argument. This is a reading of rhetorical function, not a recovered historical utterance.
- **D — Audience conduct/reception warning:** Brelochs says warnings are ignored, urges prudence or repentance, or condemns stubborn conduct. These can be hostile characterizations of an audience without containing a proposition that audience advanced against astrology.

These are evidence-status distinctions, not necessarily exclusive passage topics. They must not be collapsed into a single count of critics' accusations.

## C: Possible objections inferred from positive arguments

The following are useful interpretive hypotheses, provided they are labeled as such:

1. **Religious permission and compatibility.** In 1528 the appeal to creation, biblical/philosophical authorities, and the readable heavenly book may answer doubt over whether Christians may practice astrology: A2r 6–31 (`line-1528-1-6`–`line-1528-1-31`). The text positively licenses and dignifies the art; it does not report somebody denying that license. This concern is demonstrably thematized in the author-staged argument of 1529, but it is not thereby an attested accusation in 1528.
2. **Reliability and utility despite contingency.** In 1528 warnings may help avoid or endure harm: A2v 1–10 (`line-1528-2-1`–`line-1528-2-10`). In 1558 opposing causes and preventive practice explain why threatened events may not occur: A2v 9–29 (`line-1558-2-9`–`line-1558-2-29`). These can be read as defenses against apparent predictive failure or futility. Neither passage explicitly reports such a challenge.
3. **Causal credibility and professional standing.** In 1542 the observations of human dispositions, lunar disorders, illness, plants, magnets, purgatives, and medical authorities support real celestial effects and the physician's obligation to know the art: A1v 15–36 (`line-1542-1-15`–`line-1542-1-36`). This may answer causal skepticism and defend the author's professional competence. There is no explicit report that opponents denied the medical examples or accused Brelochs of incompetence.
4. **The legitimacy of extending ordinary heavenly effects to social events.** The 1529 transition from seasons/plants to weather, fertility, peace, and war at A1v 28–39 (`line-1529-1-28`–`line-1529-1-39`) argues by analogy. One may infer an anticipated challenge to that extension, but the text does not quote an adversary asserting it.

“Religious criticism,” “epistemological criticism,” and “professional criticism” would therefore mix observed and inferred categories unless each coded passage retained its evidence status.

| 1557 | A2r 19–23; `line-1557-1-19`–`line-1557-1-23` | Prior warnings by many were ignored, and the audience became more stubborn, as in prophetic times. | Near repetition/abridgment of the 1547 conduct complaint. It does not add a new critic charge. |

The 1547 and 1557 passages do say warnings were not heeded, so they are explicit statements about reception. They remain analytically distinct from reports about the truth claims critics advanced. They also have rhetorical universalizers (“nobody”) that should not be treated as measured audience coverage.

## Years with only brief dedication formulas

1531, 1532, 1535 (once), 1538, and 1559 contain no clear report of critics and no extended staged objection. They describe compiling a short prognostication and ask for favorable acceptance/interpretation. Requests to read it “in the best” are conventional patronal language, not sufficient evidence of active controversy. Relevant loci are 1531 A1v 6–14, 1532 A1v 6–15, 1535 A1v 7–23, 1538 A1v 7–17, and 1559 A1v 10–23/A2r 1–7. Humility about “small understanding” similarly does not establish a competence accusation.

## What a temporal argument can establish

**Strongest descriptive finding:** polemical/rhetorical emphasis varies markedly among the surviving dedication texts. There is extended authorization and mitigation in 1528; an explicit bad-reputation report plus a staged theological defense in 1529; short formulaic dedications in four subsequent observed years; renewed reported contempt and a natural/medical defense in 1542; non-reception and moral admonition in 1547 and 1557; an extended natural-theological and preventive defense in 1558; and a short dedication again in 1559.

That sequence supports a claim about **what these prefaces foreground**, not a securely demonstrated chronological transformation in what historical critics actually alleged. “Critics first attacked theological impiety, then causal credibility, then ignored warnings” would overstate the evidence at every transition: the theological issue is explicitly conditional; the later critics' substantive objection is unstated; ignored warnings concern conduct rather than an objection.

Only two distinct prefaces explicitly report adverse judgments toward the art/genre. Their content is not comparable enough to establish a trend in accusation types. A simple “early vs late” framework also misses the return to a brief dedication in 1559 and the fact that mitigation/prevention is already explicit in 1528. 1558 elaborates earlier principles rather than introducing them ex nihilo.

## Rival explanations and required cautions

- **Preface length and genre/task allocation:** a short patronal presentation has less opportunity to report or stage controversy. Silence is not evidence that criticism ceased. Complete practica might carry defense elsewhere; that is outside this independent pass.
- **Textual reuse:** 1557 recycles the 1547 warning argument. Two appearances support continuity/reuse, not two independent observations of a newly growing audience problem. Other repeated dedication formulas also reduce independence.
- **Authorial selection and strategy:** a preface can choose a defense appropriate to patronage, a didactic goal, or a rhetorical occasion without tracking changes in opponents. The texts consistently address the Hall magistracy; opposition is largely unnamed and generalized.
- **Changed circumstances versus changed criticism:** claimed long experience of wars, dearth, and disease could motivate retrospective validation and moral exhortation without a new critic proposition. Attribution to particular political/confessional events would need other primary evidence.
- **Survival and sampling:** years are irregular and missing; the texts are not annual complete coverage. No inferred gap should be filled with a claim of controversy or its absence.
- **Witness and transcription limits:** the 1535 alternate is duplicate textual evidence here, not a new occasion; leaf names can be inferred; unclear readings/first-pass transcription invite checking against images before close lexical arguments. The central “lügen büchlein,” “neydige geschwetzige,” and inclination/compulsion passages are unmarked in the TEIs, but that does not substitute for collation.
- **Source mediation:** these are Brelochs's representations of other people. His repeated authority claims, hostile epithets, conditional concessions, and sweeping claims of non-reception are rhetorical evidence. They cannot by themselves identify actual networks of critics, measure how common objections were, or prove authorial claims about correct forecasts.

The defensible research formulation is: **the surviving dedicatory prefaces change the defenses and audience responses they emphasize; whether critics' accusations changed requires additional direct evidence.**
