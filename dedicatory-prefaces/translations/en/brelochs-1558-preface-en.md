<!-- English segment 2; source range line-1558-1-7 through line-1558-2-20, inclusive. -->
Steadfast, honorable, prudent, and wise lords: first I offer Your Worships my entirely willing and diligent service. Gracious and beloved lords,

The heavenly powers have been created by divine ordinance, and they cannot be prevented from truly coming to pass and appearing. That this is so may be recognized by careful attention. Who among earthly beings can prevent the daily movements and revolutions of the heavens? What earthly person has such great power that he could reverse the course of the sun, which proceeds against the primary movement of the heavens, and turn it backward? Who could change the courses of the other planets—which at one time move forward and at another backward—into different movements? Who could have such authority that, when the sun and moon come together at the Dragon's Head or Tail, he could forbid and prevent the sun from hiding its light and radiance from us? What person has such great power that he could prevent the shadowing of the moon when it is full near the Dragon's Head or Tail? Truly, no one except almighty God, our Creator, alone.

These things are the footprints of our Redeemer, according to the testimony of the holy teacher Gregory. When we see them, we marvel not only at his strength and power, but still more at his wonderful works, wisdom, and inexpressible goodness and generosity.

Truly, they are wonderful works: through divine creation the noble lights, sun and moon, together with the other stars, have by no means been placed in the heavens in vain. These lower things are moved through the indications of the higher stars, at one time by mortality, at another by dearth, and at another by wars, etc. Such things might also appear in great number and often, if a counteracting cause of the higher bodies did not at times resist and overcome such indications.

In the same way, a magnet always draws iron to itself; but if it is smeared with garlic juice, its power is taken away. An animal's wound would also fester if it were not helped with salves and medicines. So too the stars would sometimes bring about terrible occurrences if a stronger and more powerful cause did not encounter and oppose them.

*[German passage, transcribed lines 7–48](../../tei/brelochs-1558-preface.xml#L71)*

<!-- English segment 3; source range line-1558-2-21 through line-1558-2-33, inclusive. -->
For this reason, sailors at sea always steer by the stars. Farmers and shepherds likewise observe the configuration of the moon and heavens in the conception of livestock and in the sowing and planting of crops, to avoid suffering harm from unfortunate and evil occurrences. For, as Ptolemy explains, a wise man can prevent much evil that would otherwise come through the stars if he investigates the nature and properties of the stars.

On this basis I have once again undertaken to arrange a prognostication for the coming year, as briefly as possible, indicating what good or evil the stars signify for us in that year according to the course of nature.

## Translation notes

1. The source marks only the final n of lieben in the salutation as unclear. This grammatical uncertainty does not affect the English 'beloved'. E.F.W. is rendered 'Your Worships', a collective civic honorific; Stätmeystern is rendered 'mayors'.

2. The Dragon's Head and Tail are the ascending and descending lunar nodes. The translation preserves the historical names. The 'primary movement' refers to the overarching daily motion of the heavens within the cosmology assumed by the author.

3. At A2v, lines 12–14, 'ein widerwertige vrsach der öbersten Cörper' is syntactically difficult. 'A counteracting cause of the higher bodies' closely preserves the transmitted construction. It may describe a countervailing celestial cause or a cause acting against the higher bodies' indications; the translation does not decide its exact physical location. The subsequent sentence explicitly says that a stronger cause may oppose the stars' effects.

4. The claims about garlic inhibiting magnetism and about celestial influences are translated as historical assertions. The translation does not substitute modern explanations. Sterben in the catalogue of calamities is rendered 'mortality'; the context may particularly evoke widespread deaths or epidemic mortality.

5. The translation retains the attribution to the holy teacher Gregory without supplying an unverified work or passage. The initial inability to prevent heavenly movements is distinguished in the source itself from the later possibility of counteracting their indicated earthly effects.
