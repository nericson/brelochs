<!-- English segment 2; source range line-1529-1-4 through line-1529-2-18, inclusive. -->
Steadfast, honorable, prudent, and wise sirs: my most willing and diligent service to Your Worships first of all, my gracious and beloved lords in authority. Until now, German astrological practicas have had a very bad reputation among people of every station, high and low, and have in many ways been regarded as little books of lies. Now it is certainly true that, if the art of astrology and its practitioners wished to extend their reach so far as to encroach upon the power, wisdom, and providence of the almighty Lord and God, and to confine and compel God's omnipotence within their rules, ordinances, and statutes, then no honorable, devout Christian could be surprised that such a bad reputation had arisen concerning the practica. But anyone more disposed to reflect upon the truth than to disparage severely an art of which he has neither experience nor knowledge would surely recognize that no honorable mind and heart would rise up so presumptuously against our almighty Creator and seek, through astrology or any other art and wisdom, to overpower God's eternal providence. For, according to the teaching of the holy apostle Paul, we know well that God's almighty power cannot be circumscribed, that his judgments are incomprehensible and his ways unsearchable, that no one has been his counselor, but that all things are from him, through him, and to him. We also know that everything we intend to undertake and do, in word and deed, should begin and end neither in the name of the course of the heavens nor on the basis of the wisdom of the art of astrology, but in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, whom alone God his heavenly Father has appointed and ordained to be our wisdom—indeed, a treasury of all divine knowledge.

Nevertheless, our almighty Lord and God first created the heavens by his word and power, and in the heavens the sun and moon, as well as [other?] stars; and until the end of the world he will powerfully move them and, as I might call it, cause them to revolve. Moreover, he has so finely and orderly arranged the sun and the growth of the earth in relation to one another that anyone endowed with even ordinary human understanding sees every day how all growing things on earth open joyfully in spring as the sun draws near, and fall away and wither again in winter as the sun withdraws. What, then, should prevent other similar natural occurrences on earth—namely rain, cold, warmth, fertility, crop failure, peace, and war—from likewise being aroused and brought about through the natural order of the planets created by God? This certainly does no injury to God in his divine power. He nevertheless remains the ruler and protector of those who hope in him alone and place their trust in him. He can still, independently of the course of the heavens, exalt those who humble themselves before him and humble those who exalt themselves against him. Indeed, so far from anything being taken away from God's power as exercised in his ordinary order, the true and genuine art of astrology rather ascribes that power to almighty God. For true, godly astrology is nothing other than a rule and measuring line of God's ordinary working, which almighty God carries out and accomplishes through his own creation, the heavens and the stars. This does not mean that he has confined and sealed himself within that order as a prisoner, but that, according to his customary course and order, he works and acts through the highest things in this world down to the lowest.

Therefore no devout or upright person need fear the stars or their threats, as the prophet Jeremiah also testifies. The stars certainly portend no evil to the devout; they are also those whose duty it is to fear God their Lord alone. The wicked, however, must always be on their guard, whether the stars are favorable or unfavorable. For even if the stars portend great good fortune in this world, what use is that if through their wickedness they turn such good fortune to their own misfortune? Since this does no injury at all to divine power, but rather explains it in its ordered operation, I have accordingly wished, following the teaching and counsel of those experienced in this art, to make known the inclinations and influences of the stars, signs, and planets for the coming year—not as things that must necessarily happen, but as a warning—and have given them for dissemination in print, for the particular pleasure of Your Worships. In this matter and at all times, I wish to commend myself to Your Worships, my gracious lords in authority, subject to you in humble service and obedience. Given on the day of John the Baptist, the twenty-fourth of June, in the year 1528.

## Translation notes

1. E.F.W., a conventional civic honorific, is rendered 'Your Worships'; the letters and forms of address vary in the source. 'Mayors' renders Stetmeyster. Leybartzet is rendered 'physician' in the subscription; the source identifies an appointed personal physician serving the civic authorities, without specifying a modern municipal office structure.

2. Astronomey is translated 'astrology' where it names the art defended in this preface. The historical term embraces knowledge of celestial motions and their supposed terrestrial influences; the modern separation of astronomy and astrology should not be imposed on Brelochs's vocabulary. Practica is retained as the name of the prognostication genre.

3. The source's unclear vnndere in A1v, line 29 is provisionally understood as 'other' and rendered '[other?]'. This translation does not constitute a correction of the German working transcription.

4. Fürsichtigkeit when applied to God is translated 'providence'. Ordentlich in the discussion of divine power and action is rendered with 'ordinary' or 'ordered' language: Brelochs distinguishes God's customary operation through creation from a limitation of his freedom. No claim is made here that the wording is a formal deployment of the scholastic potentia ordinata/absoluta distinction.

5. The references to Paul's teaching evoke Romans 11:33–36; the statements concerning Christ and knowledge also recall 1 Corinthians 1:30 and Colossians 2:3. The reference to Jeremiah most naturally recalls Jeremiah 10:2. These are editorial identifications of allusions rather than references supplied in the printed text. The translation follows Brelochs's wording rather than substituting a modern Bible version.

6. Brachmonat is rendered June. The dedication is dated June 24, 1528, although the forecast year is 1529. The entire continuous body paragraph is encoded as one source-aligned segment, with editorial paragraph breaks in English, to avoid assigning translated clauses to inaccurate line boundaries where the printed German runs across them.
