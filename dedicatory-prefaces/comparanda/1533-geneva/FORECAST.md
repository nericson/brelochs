# The Geneva forecast for 1533 in English

Anton Brelochs, *Practique ou pronostication nouvelle Dallemaigne, et almanach véritable*. Geneva, Gabriel Pomar, [1532]. Bibliothèque de Genève, Bsa 46. This working translation follows the reconstructed booklet in reading order. The forecast year is 1533. Month names, numerals and punctuation are modernized. Uncertain readings are noted. The numbered chapters and the order of all the tables come from the print. ## Title and calendar · [A1]r

A new forecast or prognostication from Germany, and a true almanac, composed by the expert and renowned Master Antoine Breelochs, a German, doctor of medicine in the noble city of Hall in German lands. Translated from German into French for the year 1533.

**Mars and Jupiter, lords of this year.** Two woodcuts represent the planets. Mars holds a spear and shield. Jupiter holds a staff and three arrows. Between the figures, below, stands the word *Almanach*.

The golden number is 14, the solar cycle 2, the Sunday letter E and the Roman indiction 6. Christmas to Shrove Sunday is eight weeks and four days. Candlemas to Shrovetide is twenty-two days.

## Brelochs to the reader · [A1]v

**1**  Antoine Brelochs sends greetings to the reader.

**2–8**  I cannot marvel enough at the minds and thoughts of the people of our day, fallen into such great ignorance. They would sooner dwell on things down here and set their hearts and minds on them than on heavenly things. Surely we see God’s anger against us, the heavens in motion, and people’s minds going out of their senses. They rise against us like reckless people in a rage.

**8–13**  Even so, we are unwilling to correct and amend the understanding and counsel of our predecessors. We would sooner give free rein to the inclinations of the heavens, which have force and vigor in us, asking none of God’s grace or help until we see ourselves and our possessions brought to nothing by our enemies.

**13–15**  But listen, I pray you. This Ptolemy [possibly “this pagan Ptolemy”], that is, the prince of astrologers, says in his Almagest [reading uncertain] that the wise man will be lord and master of the stars and planets.

**15–20**  Surely a prudent physician skilled in the signs of the heavens recognizes, from signs of anger, hot and dry blood boiling in the human body. Will he not immediately change the quality of such a body to coldness and moisture, so that this force can no longer exert itself and there can instead be a proper balance, in which all our health consists?

**21–23**  In the same way the fury of adversaries can readily be appeased through circumspection and wisdom. Anyone, however slow of understanding, can readily recognize this from what has been said.

**24–31**  These things being so, moved more by pity for the commonwealth than by vainglory, I have wished to draw out some obscure matters from the teachings and rules of learned astrologers. These show us clearly how, in this year 1533, God’s fragile vessels will be led by the various motions of the heavens. Once these evils are foreseen, we can readily avoid them through human prudence, to the praise of God. Such praise I desire from the depths of my heart for all true Christians.

**32–34**  I ask you to receive this briefly worded prognostication in good part, taking and interpreting it in the spirit in which I wrote it. I bid you farewell.

## The lunar eclipse of this year · [A1]v

This year the moon will lose its brightness on the fourth day of August, at the third point, at ten hours and seven minutes, in the twenty-first degree of Aquarius. Half of it will be covered at eleven hours and fifty-three minutes after noon. The end will come at night, at one hour and thirty-nine minutes. It will exert its greatest force on the ninth of November and last until the twenty-fourth of December. From then it will diminish until 4 March 1534.

The phrase “at the third point” retains the printed wording. The last lines cross a damaged area. These are the times and effects claimed by the forecaster. The August full-moon entry below gives its own time, 11:57 after noon.

## 1. The planets governing the world this year · [A2]r

If we carefully consider the strength of all seven planets in the four parts of this year, we shall find that most cruel Mars and Jupiter his assistant are stronger than all the others. With good reason they should therefore be called the lords and princes of this year. Created things here on earth will be disposed chiefly according to the courses of those two planets. The other planets also have their part. Sometimes they will increase the working and effect of those two, and sometimes indeed lessen it, as what follows will show more clearly.

## 2. The harmful diseases and infirmities of this year · [A2]r

From the conjunction of influences and the properties of the stars I find many grievous and life-threatening ills this year. There will be headaches of various kinds, dangerous abscesses of the mouth, painful eyes, wounds, fistulas and poisonous ulcers. There will be hot illnesses and dangerous, hot, acute pestilential fevers. These diseases will prevail among humans and beasts alike. There will also be severe twisting pains and disorders of the belly, including intestinal obstruction, colic, stomach pain, coughing up blood, dropsy, and diseases of the bowels and abdomen. Albumasar says that when Mars is found in Aquarius at the beginning of the year, it signifies much human sickness owing to harsh winds and an abundance of bad blood, especially perilous for little children.

## 3. The wars and defenses of this year · [A2]r

I turn to the strength and vigor of the planets, particularly to the influences and operations of Mars, prince of this year. I find terrible battles and great bloodshed this year, more than in all the years past. Albumasar bears witness to these things. When Mars, lord of the year, is found under Aquarius at the angle of the earth, it signifies killings and battles, great tumult and unrest among the people, death and bloodshed, and the forgetting of pity in human hearts. While Mars is in this sign, battles will come because people demand justice. These troubles will endure a long time. The people of some lands will be plundered by paid soldiers. Those toward the west and north and in certain parts of Germany are in such peril.

In the *Book of Great Conjunctions* Albumasar says that Mars in Aquarius signifies that the king of the East will be slain along with a great multitude of his people. Others cite “Raguel,” who says that the moon in the twelfth house under Virgo threatens the lands and regions of Greece with battle and divisions. The Geneva spelling of the authority’s name is retained here.

## 4. The fruits and produce of the earth · [A2]v

The influences of the planets gravely threaten the fruits of the earth this year, chiefly in January, March, April, May and September. Yet in the regions of the east and west on the left side, and also toward the north, they will exercise their malice more gently. Saturn does not cast an adverse aspect on Jupiter, and the moon is found in Virgo, counteracting the great circuit. By the rules of Albumasar we can readily conclude that this year is disposed more toward fertility than barrenness. There will be wine in sufficient abundance everywhere, to the great joy of drinkers, and a middling supply of wheat, oats and other grains. In June there is reason to fear that hail will ruin them. The fruits of trees will suffer great damage from worms and locusts.

## 5. Of certain orders of people in general · [A2]v–[A3]r

**Saturn.** Rich old people, black monks, peasants, furriers, tailors, shoemakers and, in sum, all born under Saturn will earn little from their business this year. They will also suffer severe diseases of the chest, coughs, dangerous abscesses of the side and bowels, and hot, acute fevers, chiefly in January and May.

**Jupiter.** Prelates in religious orders, those who preside over the church, judges and all those born under Jupiter will be in danger of severe disorders of the head, the organs of generation and the legs, as well as harassment in warfare, particularly in May. This is because of last year’s solar eclipse in their house and other adverse influences. The genital reading translates the unusual printed *genitrix* provisionally.

**Mars.** Commanders of armies, the sons of Mars, captains, knights and paid soldiers must use their reason and be on their guard. Mars will suddenly take many of them. Some will suffer in prison, others will be driven out by their enemies. Tertian fevers, bowel diseases and poisonous abscesses threaten them, especially in January, May and August.

**The Sun.** Kings, princes, those of noble descent and all those under the planet of the Sun will be inclined to battles and dissensions on account of some of their subjects, with danger of death in March, July, August, October and November. The French leaves the reference of the last relative clause loose.

**Venus.** Women, maidens, widows, poets, orators, musicians and, in short, all those born under Venus will suffer much from deadly diseases and human enmity in January, February, April, June, July, August, September, October, November and December. Once this time has passed they will enjoy great prosperity and good fortune. Pregnant women will give birth easily and with a favorable outcome.

**Mercury.** Masters, students in the arts, scribes, calculators, all people of great understanding, and children who stammer, born under Mercury, should take care, especially in April, May, June, July, August and September. Their sign is in a bad position and shows them battles, sickness and a high cost of living. [Beginning of the leaf](forecast-A3r.jpg)

**The Moon.** The common people, notaries, carters, servants and everyone born under the moon will encounter adverse fortune. Public and private quarrels will break out among them. Friendship will cease, so that many will end their lives in poverty and misery.

## 6. Of certain kingdoms, lands, regions, territories and cities · [A3]r

**Germany, Poland, “la minor,” Florence, Constance.** Let them beware of foreign guests. They have good reason to fear dissensions and battles, for mortal sickness will come upon them and pestilential fever will hold sway. The syntax of the place list is uncertain. “La minor” may qualify Poland, in contrast to Greater Poland in the next group. The impression appears to run it into *florence*.

**Greater Poland, Sechlesia, Saxony, Thuringia, Franconia, Fussia, Breussia, Haluernia and Bononia.** In spring they will suffer grievous and life-threatening ills and will have little peace from great disputes and quarrels. At other times this year their fortune will be middling. Uncertain place-names here and below retain forms close to the print.

**England, Flanders, Lombardy, Paris and Halle.** If they fail to use reason and prudence, they will be caught up in great misfortunes, now through the enmities of religious people, now through severe choleric illnesses. These will bring death to many of them.

**Italy, Scotland, Turkey, Rome, Prague, Ulm and Mantua.** They will suffer adversity through various battles and deadly diseases. Some rulers of those regions will die.

**The Dauphiné, Swabia, Bavaria, Austria, Vienna, Speyer, Heidelberg, Salzburg, Nuremberg, Frankfurt and Regensburg.** Some will be subject to pestilential fevers, others to dangerous diseases. Many of the cities named above will face battles, want and a scarcity of the fruits of the earth owing to the poisonous rays of Saturn, who will especially make sport of them this year.

**Spain, Hungary, Moranie, Granacia, Madeburga, Venda, Stucardia and Lubera.** They will pass the year with middling fortune. Even so, they will have to be governed with foresight, lest some harm come from strange serpents, such as pains of the head and eyes. The syntax of the last warning is difficult.

## 7. The four seasons, the qualities of the twelve months and the different aspects of the sun and moon · [A3]r–[A4]r

Winter, beginning on the first day of January and lasting until the tenth day of March, will be cold and wet, with abundant snow and harsh winds. On the Thursday after the Circumcision of our Lord the weather will turn cold, tending to snow. On the Feast of the Three Kings there will be a cold wind, with rain in warm regions. In cold regions it will not last until the following Thursday.

The reconstructed booklet proceeds directly from winter to the twelve monthly entries below. There are no separate prose passages for spring, summer or autumn in this copy.

## Closing prayer, colophon and motto · [A4]r

And so ends my forecast, drawn from the natural influences of the constellations to give people warning. For this reason we shall all pray devoutly to God that he may turn every misfortune away from us. Amen.

Translated and printed at Geneva by Gabriel Pomar.

*In te, Domine, confido.* In you, Lord, I trust.

Two angels support the arms of Geneva beneath the motto. The reverse of the leaf is blank. The French colophon places both activities under Pomar’s name.
